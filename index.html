The Core Engineering Trade-Off:

In space, you can never produce something from nothing. Generating oxygen consumes electrical power; growing food takes water and lighting; shielding against radiation drains energy for electromagnetic coils or heavy regolith rovers. Keep all 4 bars above zero to survive!

🚀 Real NASA Technology Behind AstroBase

1. ECLSS (Environmental Control and Life Support System)

Aboard the International Space Station and future Artemis habitats, NASA's ECLSS recycles up to 98% of all water and scrubs carbon dioxide using amine beds and Sabatier reactors to generate breathable oxygen.

2. MOXIE (Mars Oxygen In-Situ Resource Utilization Experiment)

Aboard NASA's Perseverance rover on Mars, MOXIE proved that atmospheric carbon dioxide (CO₂) can be electrochemically split into breathable oxygen (O₂) at high temperatures (800°C), paving the way for future Martian outposts.

3. Space Power: Kilopower & Stirling Nuclear Fission

Solar panels can be blinded by 14-day lunar polar nights and global Martian dust storms. NASA's Kilopower project uses small, lightweight nuclear fission reactors to provide 1 to 10 kilowatts of constant, dust-independent power.

4. Regolith Radiation Shielding

Deep space radiation (Galactic Cosmic Rays and Solar Particle Events) carries dangerous ionizing doses. NASA Artemis plans call for burying human habitats under 2 to 3 meters of compacted lunar soil (regolith) or using 3D-printed sintered sulfur-regolith shells.
